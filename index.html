The AI data centre boom

Australia's AI build-out is happening in four states. Be part of it.

Australia is now the world's second-largest destination for data centre investment, and AI is the biggest driver. Pick a state to see what's being built.

Western Australia

14 operating · 6 more in the pipeline
  • NEXTDC leads the build-out locally, with new capacity like the CDC Maddington campus (200MW) in development.
  • The Pilbara is emerging as a hub for renewably-powered AI data centres, with miners including Fortescue exploring gigawatt-scale campuses near Port Hedland.
  • Cheap renewable energy and industrial land mean more builds, more shifts and more crews needed on the ground.

New South Wales

120+ data centres · the country's largest market
  • Led by AirTrunk, CDC, NEXTDC, Equinix and Global Switch across Sydney.
  • Macquarie Park is now joined by a fast-growing Western Sydney corridor: Eastern Creek, Kemps Creek and the Aerotropolis.
  • Aerotropolis investment proposals more than doubled to $21.6B in under two years, with new substations and the airport metro landing through 2027.

Victoria

630MW+ AirTrunk capacity across MEL1 & MEL2 by 2027
  • NEXTDC's roughly $2 billion M4 campus in Port Melbourne has been fast-tracked by the Victorian Government.
  • AirTrunk's MEL2 adds 354MW+ in the north-west, lifting its Melbourne investment past $7 billion.
  • Truganina, Mickleham and Donnybrook are emerging as Melbourne's own hyperscale corridor, with the committed pipeline set to almost triple live capacity.

Queensland

25 operating · 5 more in the pipeline
  • NEXTDC's B2 Brisbane expansion is doubling capacity from 6MW to 12MW as part of a $160 million investment creating hundreds of construction roles.
  • The Sunshine Coast is emerging as a secondary hub outside the Brisbane CBD.
  • With the 2032 Olympics, digital infrastructure is being built alongside a wave of transport, venue and energy projects, all competing for the same skilled workforce.

About these figures

Nationally, Australia's data centre pipeline is worth an estimated $150–155 billion through 2030 (around six gigawatts of capacity, roughly four times what's operating today) and is temporarily supporting some 400,000 jobs as it's built and switched on. AI-related data centres alone now account for roughly 17% of Australia's total private investment. Sources: CommBank, Westpac IQ, Cushman & Wakefield, Baxtel/Datacenters.com market data, AirTrunk, iTWire, CRN, TechRepublic, DatacenterDynamics, RenewMap, 2026.
