ACMA cabling registration explained.

Open, Restricted or Lift? What each covers, what you need to get it, and why data centre employers keep asking for it.

Join the talent network →Browse live jobs

If you've looked at data centre or commercial electrical jobs lately, you've probably seen "ACMA Open cabling registration" or "registered cabler" in the requirements. Here's what it is, which type you need, and how to get it.

What is cabling registration?

In Australia, anyone who installs or supervises telecommunications cabling (data, phone, structured cabling and similar) on customer premises must be a registered cabling provider. The rules are set by the Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA) under the Telecommunications Act, in the Telecommunications Cabling Provider Rules, which were remade in 2025.

Your electrical licence doesn't cover this work on its own. It's a separate registration, and it's one of the most common extras on data centre job ads.

Open vs Restricted vs Lift

There are three kinds of cabling registration:

TypeWhat it coversExperience needed
OpenThe broadest registration. Covers cabling work in commercial and domestic premises, and specialised cabling once you've completed the extra competencies.At least 360 hours of relevant cabling experience
RestrictedA narrower set of work, mainly simpler cabling in domestic premises.At least 80 hours of relevant cabling experience
LiftOnly cabling related to lifts and elevators.Specific to lift work

For data centre and commercial work, you want Open. Restricted won't cover the structured cabling in a commercial building or data hall.

Specialised cabling: fibre and structured

On top of your registration category, some cabling work needs extra competencies. Examples include optical fibre, coaxial and broadband cabling, and aerial cable. Under the 2025 rules, an Open-registered cabler can do any type of specialised cabling, as long as they've completed the competency and registration requirements for it.

Data centre ads often ask for Open registration "with structured and fibre endorsements". In practice, that means Open registration plus the specialised competencies for that kind of work.

How to get registered

  1. Do the training. Complete a registered training course for the category you want, through a registered training organisation. Registrars generally expect you to pass the mandatory cabling regulations exam with at least 80%.
  2. Log your experience. You'll need at least 360 hours of relevant cabling experience for Open registration, or 80 hours for Restricted. At least one registrar requires these hours within a two-year period. Registrars provide templates to document your hours.
  3. Apply to a registrar. The ACMA doesn't issue registrations itself. A small number of ACMA-accredited registrars handle applications, renewals and recognition of prior learning. Fees vary by registrar; one charges about $92 for three years.
  4. Keep it current. Renew before it expires, and update your registration when you add specialised competencies or move from Restricted to Open.

Already an electrician?

If you're a licensed electrician who wants to do communications cabling, you'll need Restricted or Open registration. Your electrical work experience may count towards the experience requirement, so check with your registrar before you start logging hours from scratch.

Why it matters for your job prospects

Data centres are full of data and fibre, so employers like electricians who can do both. Open registration with fibre and structured competencies won't replace data centre experience, but it's one of the quickest ways to make your CV stand out for fit-out, technician and commissioning roles. It pairs well with the other tickets in our guide on how to become a data centre electrician.

This guide is general information only, based on ACMA and registrar information current at November 2026. Rules and fees change, so confirm current requirements with the ACMA and your chosen cabling registrar before you enrol or apply.

Frequently asked questions

Do electricians need ACMA cabling registration?

Your electrical licence doesn't cover telecommunications cabling work. To install or supervise data, phone or structured cabling on customer premises you need ACMA cabling registration, either Restricted or Open.

What is the difference between Open and Restricted cabling registration?

Open is the broadest registration and covers commercial and domestic premises, plus specialised cabling once you meet the extra competencies. Restricted covers a narrower set of mainly domestic work. Open needs at least 360 hours of relevant cabling experience; Restricted needs at least 80 hours.

Who issues ACMA cabling registration?

The ACMA doesn't register cablers directly. A small number of ACMA-accredited cabling registrars manage registrations, renewals and recognition of prior learning on its behalf.

Do data centre jobs need cabling registration?

Not every role, but many data centre fit-out, technician and commissioning roles list ACMA Open cabling registration, often with structured cabling and optical fibre competencies, as essential or highly regarded.
