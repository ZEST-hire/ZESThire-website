How to become a data centre electrician in Australia.

The licences, tickets and experience you need to move into data centre work, and how to land your first role.

Join the talent network →Browse live jobs

Data centres are hiring electricians faster than almost any other sector in Australia. If you're already licensed, the jump is smaller than most people think. Here's what you need and how to make the move.

1. Get (or keep) your electrical licence current

Every data centre electrician job starts with a full electrical licence for the state you'll be working in. The usual path is a four-year electrotechnology apprenticeship and a Certificate III in Electrotechnology Electrician, followed by your state licence. Licences are issued by each state's regulator:

  • Western Australia: Building and Energy, part of the WA Government
  • New South Wales: NSW Fair Trading
  • Queensland: the Electrical Safety Office
  • Victoria: Energy Safe Victoria, after passing the Licensed Electrician's Assessment (LEA)

If you're moving interstate, check the mutual recognition rules with the regulator in your new state before you start applying.

2. Add ACMA cabling registration

Data centres are full of data and fibre, and registered cablers are required for telecommunications and data cabling work. Cabling registration is managed through ACMA-accredited registrars, with different categories (such as Open and Restricted) and extra competency requirements for specialised work like structured cabling, optical fibre and coaxial. Plenty of data centre roles, especially in fit-out and technician work, list an Open registration with structured and fibre endorsements as essential or highly regarded. If you don't have it, it's one of the quickest ways to make your CV stand out. Our guide to ACMA cabling registration explains how to get it.

3. Line up the common site tickets

Requirements vary by site, but these come up again and again in data centre job ads:

  • White card (general construction induction)
  • Working at heights
  • EWP (elevating work platform)
  • Low voltage rescue (LVR) and CPR, kept current
  • First aid, and confined space for some plant rooms
  • Police check or site security clearance for some operators

4. Build the experience that translates

You don't need to have worked in a data centre to get your first data centre job. Employers regularly hire electricians from:

  • Commercial construction: cable tray, containment, switchboards and large fit-outs
  • Industrial and mining: high-consequence environments, permits and isolations
  • Telecommunications: structured cabling and fibre
  • Hospitals and critical facilities: UPS, generators and essential services

On your CV, call out anything involving UPS, generators, ATS, switchgear, BMS, commissioning, live environments or permit-to-work systems. That's what data centre hiring managers scan for.

5. Pick your path: construction, shift or commissioning

Construction roles are the easiest entry point, especially during big hall releases. Shift roles in live facilities offer stability and rotating rosters but often ask for some critical-environment experience. Commissioning is a great step up once you've been on a few projects. Read more about the differences on our data centre electrician jobs page, or see how the roles compare in data centre technician vs electrician.

6. Get in front of the right people

A lot of data centre work moves through referrals and specialist recruiters rather than job boards. Join a talent network, keep your tickets current, and be clear about your roster, rate and location preferences. That way, when a role comes up you're the first call.

This guide is general information only. Licensing and registration rules change, so always confirm current requirements with your state electrical regulator and an ACMA-accredited cabling registrar.

Frequently asked questions

Can I become a data centre electrician without data centre experience?

Yes. Many electricians move into data centres from commercial construction, industrial, mining and telecommunications work. Construction and fit-out roles are usually the easiest entry point.

Do data centre electricians need ACMA cabling registration?

Not every role requires it, but many fit-out and technician roles list ACMA cabling registration, often with structured cabling and optical fibre endorsements, as essential or highly regarded.

Who issues electrical licences in Australia?

Each state has its own regulator: Building and Energy in WA, NSW Fair Trading in NSW, the Electrical Safety Office in Queensland and Energy Safe Victoria in Victoria.
