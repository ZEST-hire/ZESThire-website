Why electricians are moving into data centres

Data centres have become one of the best places in Australia to be a licensed electrician. There's a long pipeline of construction work, operations roles offer stable rosters in clean, air-conditioned environments, and the skills you build (UPS, generators, switchgear, BMS, commissioning) open doors to supervisor, technician and facilities careers.

The three main types of data centre electrician job

  • Construction electrician. Working for an electrical contractor on a new hall or campus: cable tray, cable pulling, terminations, switchboards and busway. Usually project-based, often with overtime.
  • Shift or facilities electrician. Working in a live facility for the operator or its facilities management provider, maintaining critical power and responding to alarms. Commonly on 12-hour rotating rosters such as 4 on, 4 off.
  • Commissioning electrician or technician. Testing and proving systems before handover, from pre-functional checks through to integrated systems testing. Great experience if you want to move into technician or engineering-adjacent roles.

What employers usually look for

  • A current electrical licence for the state you'll work in
  • Commercial, industrial, mining or telco experience (direct data centre experience is a bonus, not always essential)
  • White card, plus tickets like working at heights, EWP and LVR/CPR
  • ACMA cabling registration with structured and fibre endorsements for some roles
  • A clean safety record and comfort working to permits and method statements

Frequently asked questions

Do I need data centre experience to get a data centre electrician job?

Not always. Many employers will consider licensed electricians with solid commercial, industrial, mining or telecommunications experience, especially for construction roles. Live operations roles more often ask for critical-environment experience.

What licences do I need to be a data centre electrician?

You need a current electrical licence for the state you're working in. Many roles also ask for a white card, working at heights and EWP tickets, LVR/CPR, and some ask for ACMA cabling registration with structured cabling and optical fibre endorsements.

What rosters do data centre electricians work?

Construction roles are usually day work with overtime. Live operations roles are often 24/7 shift rosters, such as 12-hour shifts on a 4 on, 4 off pattern.
