Data centre technician vs electrician: what’s the difference?

Same buildings, very different days. How the main data centre roles compare, and which one suits you.

Join the talent network →Browse live jobs

Search data centre jobs in Australia and you'll see a lot of similar-sounding titles: data centre technician, critical environment technician, service technician, critical operations technician, shift electrician. They overlap, but they're not the same job. Here's how to tell them apart and work out which one fits you.

The short version

What a data centre electrician does

A data centre electrician is a licensed electrician working on the electrical side of the facility. On a new build that means cable tray and containment, pulling and terminating LV power, switchboards, busway and lighting across new data halls. In a live facility it means planned and reactive maintenance on switchboards, UPS systems, generators and critical power, usually under strict permits and method statements.

You need a full electrical licence for the state you work in, and the work is mostly on the tools. Pay is usually quoted as an hourly rate on construction and as a salary or hourly rate plus shift penalties in live sites. See our data centre electrician salary guide for current figures.

What a data centre technician does

A data centre technician (often called a critical environment, service or critical operations technician) looks after the whole facility while it runs. Typical duties include:

  • Monitoring power, cooling, fire and building management systems
  • Being first to respond to alarms, working out how serious they are and fixing or escalating
  • Routine checks and preventive maintenance on electrical, mechanical and fire systems
  • Managing and escorting contractors and vendors on site
  • Following and writing operating procedures, switching plans and incident reports

The work is broader and less tools-heavy than an electrician's. You're a generalist across electrical, mechanical and controls, and the job is as much about judgement, procedures and communication as about the tools.

Who gets hired as a technician?

Operators are open about wanting tradespeople. Current Australian technician ads commonly say electrical, HVAC or mechanical trades are preferred, that data centre experience isn't essential, and that in-house training is provided. They pitch the role at qualified electricians and HVAC technicians who want to step off the tools and into operations.

More senior operations technician roles typically ask for two or more years in a critical environment, and they count hospitals, utilities, defence and industrial plants as well as data centres.

Rosters and pay

Both roles often run on 24/7 rotating rosters in live facilities, for example 2 days, 2 nights, then 4 days off. Shift loadings make these roles some of the better-paid operations jobs around: one current 24/7 service technician role in Canberra is advertised at AUD 130,800 to 141,600. Construction electricians on hourly rates with overtime can earn as much or more during busy build phases, but the work moves from project to project.

Which one should you go for?

  • Pick electrician if you love the tools, want project variety and overtime, or want to move into HV, commissioning or supervising crews.
  • Pick technician if you want a stable roster in one facility, less physical work, broader systems knowledge and a path into facilities or operations management.
  • Can't decide? Plenty of people start as a data centre electrician on a build, then move into a technician role once the site goes live. The electrical licence keeps both doors open.

Frequently asked questions

Is a data centre technician the same as an electrician?

No. A data centre electrician is a licensed electrician doing hands-on electrical work. A data centre technician (also called a critical environment or service technician) keeps a live facility running across power, cooling, fire and monitoring systems, responding to alarms and coordinating maintenance.

Do you need an electrical licence to be a data centre technician?

Not always. Many operators prefer an electrical, HVAC or mechanical trade, and a licence widens your options, but some technician roles train people with other technical backgrounds.

Can an electrician become a data centre technician?

Yes. Australian operators actively target licensed electricians and HVAC technicians who want to move off the tools and into operations, and many provide in-house training.

What roster do data centre technicians work?

Many work 24/7 rotating rosters, such as 2 days, 2 nights then 4 days off, though business-hours technician roles also exist.
